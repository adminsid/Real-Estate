# Portal trust and litigation lessons

Research date: November 15, 2026. Internal product research, with candidate public copy clearly separated below. Scope: inquiry routing, representation, paid placement, rental syndication, lender steering, and touring agreements. Sources are agency materials, statutes, SEC filings, and actual court documents; court documents hosted by counsel or a party remain pleadings/orders, not independent commentary. This is not a complete docket audit or a legal opinion about this platform.

## What the primary sources establish

### 1. Buyer-contact confusion and mortgage steering: Taylor / Armstrong

**Allegations:** The December 29, 2025 consolidated complaint alleged that a buyer using “Contact Agent” believed he was contacting a property's listing agent but was routed to a Zillow partner. These are plaintiffs' allegations, not established facts. [Consolidated complaint, document 45, p. 72](https://www.cohenmilstein.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2025.12.29-Taylor-v.-Zillow-Consolidated-Amended-Complaint.pdf).

**Court ruling:** In *Taylor v. Zillow*, W.D. Washington, 2:25-cv-01818-JLR, Judge James L. Robart dismissed the consolidated complaint **with leave to amend on July 27, 2026**. On the contact-agent theory, the court found the pleaded lack-of-notice theory implausible in light of disclosures and inadequate allegations of deceptive interactions. The order permitted a further complaint by August 17. This was a pleading decision, not a trial determination of all portal conduct. [Order, document 102, pp. 2, 20–21, 32](https://assets.ctfassets.net/hzfwsdcegxo2/6xMBOQpnI2y45gd8YKou1L/eeca61203a945804bc8c92fcf68beb48/Taylor_v_Zillow_dismissed_July_27.pdf).

**Later primary filing:** Plaintiffs filed document 103, a consolidated third amended complaint, on **August 17, 2026**, focusing on alleged exchanges of buyer leads for mortgage referrals and asserting RESPA and Washington consumer-protection claims. Filing a replacement complaint does not establish those allegations. [Filed third amended complaint](https://www.hbsslaw.com/sites/default/files/case-downloads/zillow-agent-class-action/2026-08-17-consolidated-third-amended-class-action-complaint.pdf).

**Company response, separately:** Zillow's July 27 statement, updated August 17, denies the claims and says its tools and lender choices are optional and disclosed. Its headline should not be used to imply the litigation ended finally: the update itself acknowledges the new complaint. [Zillow statement](https://www.zillow.com/news/court-dismisses-baseless-taylor-v-zillow-lawsuit/).

**Status limit:** The August 17 complaint is the latest merits-related primary Taylor filing inspected for this note. No subsequent final disposition was verified. Do not publish an unqualified “case dismissed” or a precise November 15 motion status without refreshing the docket.

**Product inference:** Put recipient and role disclosures next to the relevant action, and align them with actual routing. Do not label a brokerage inquiry “Contact listing agent” unless that is the recipient. A factual “Request help from Prime America” is clearer where the inquiry goes to this brokerage.

### 2. Agent antitrust case: Dupuis is a separate proceeding

**Allegations:** *Dupuis et al. v. Zillow Group et al.*, W.D. Washington, 3:26-cv-05049-JLR, challenges alleged conditioning of agent referrals on purchasing Follow Up Boss and steering clients to Zillow Home Loans. The amended complaint was filed **May 4, 2026**, document 27. [Filed amended complaint](https://s3.amazonaws.com/hoth.bizango/assets/31515/0027_Pl_Amended_Complaint_050426_1_.pdf).

**Verified ruling:** On **September 28, 2026**, document 37, Judge Robart denied Zillow's motion to dismiss, allowing pleaded antitrust, consumer-protection, and unjust-enrichment theories to proceed. A finding that claims are sufficiently pleaded is not a finding that the alleged conduct occurred or that Zillow is liable. This ruling did not decide Taylor. [Order, especially pp. 6–7, 35](https://s3.amazonaws.com/hoth.bizango/assets/32105/Order_Denying_Def_Motion_Dismiss_092826.pdf).

**Company response, separately:** Zillow's September 29 update says the court had not ruled on the merits and disputes the claims, maintaining that consumers choose their agent and lender. [Official statement and dated updates](https://www.zillow.com/news/zillow-challenges-dupuis-complaint-says-it-fails-to-state-any-claims-in-motion-to-dismiss/).

**Status limit:** The September 28 order is the latest court order inspected here; no final merits judgment was verified. Later procedural reporting is not treated as a verified court finding in this note.

**Product inference:** Keep buyer help separate from lender selection. Do not reward routing or recommend a lender on the basis of an undisclosed commercial relationship. Any future referral fee, required software, or lender target needs review of actual terms and conduct, not merely a better disclaimer.

### 3. FTC and states v. Zillow / Redfin rental advertising

**Agency allegations and remedy:** The FTC filed its complaint **September 30, 2025**, alleging a February 2025 agreement removed Redfin as an independent competitor in multifamily rental internet-listing advertising. On **August 24, 2026**, the FTC announced a stipulated resolution with Arizona, Connecticut, New York, Virginia, and Washington. The announced remedy removes competitive restrictions, requires Redfin to restart independent advertising operations, and permits continued Zillow listing syndication. It does not prohibit syndication generally. [FTC case timeline](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/zillow-groupredfin-corp), [FTC resolution announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/08/ftc-secures-order-resolving-antitrust-concerns-zillow-redfin-agreement).

**Terms versus liability:** The published stipulated order states the settlement was reached without trial or final adjudication of disputed issues, records defendants' denials, and provides that it is not an admission of wrongdoing. [Order, pp. 1–2, hosted by New York AG](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/settlements-agreements/virginia-et-al-v-zillow-group-inc-et-al-stipulated-final-order-2026.pdf).

**Latest company filing:** Zillow's quarter-ended September 30, 2026 Form 10-Q reports that the order was entered **August 28, 2026** and fully resolved the FTC/state matter. The FTC landing page still displays “Pending,” so do not rely on that badge alone. The same filing describes Premier Agent leads sold through performance fees or advertising share of voice; paid distribution itself is a disclosed business model, not proof of unlawful conduct. [SEC filing, Note 11 and Revenue Overview](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617640/000161764026000069/z-20260930.htm).

**Product inference:** Identify feed provenance, freshness, and listing coverage honestly. Do not claim “all listings” merely because several partner sites display the same feed. Do not present payment-driven agent placement as an independent quality ranking. Syndication and paid advertising require attention to the actual arrangement; neither is automatically unlawful.

### 4. Touring agreements: NAR rules and New York law are different sources

NAR states that, effective **August 17, 2024**, MLS participants working with a buyer need a written agreement before an in-person or live virtual home tour. Its consumer guide says independent open-house visits and asking about an agent's services do not require such an agreement; compensation and other terms are negotiable. These are settlement-related industry practice requirements, not a universal statute requiring every website visitor to sign. [NAR Written Buyer Agreements 101](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/written-buyer-agreements-101), [NAR consumer guide](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-to-written-buyer-agreements).

New York **Real Property Law §443** separately governs agency disclosure. Its statutory disclosure form expressly states “THIS IS NOT A CONTRACT” and explains whose interests an agent represents. Do not merge receipt of this form, a service inquiry, and a representation agreement into one unexplained consent. [Current §443 text](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/443).

New York **S9564**, introduced March 25, 2026, would require signed buyer-broker agreements before buyer-agent services. The official page still identifies it as in Senate Judiciary Committee, rather than enacted law. Do not describe that proposal as a current statewide touring requirement. [Official bill status and text](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9564).

## Candidate public copy

Use operational statements only once the interface and actual brokerage workflow support them. The copy below expresses intended behavior, not a verified description of today's implementation.

**Short trust statement:**

> We want you to understand who you are contacting and what happens next. Before you send an inquiry, we identify the brokerage receiving it and explain the agent's role. You can ask questions before deciding whom to work with. We explain any representation agreement and compensation before asking you to sign.

**If the inquiry routes to this brokerage rather than the listing agent:**

> Your request goes to Prime America Real Estate for help with this property. The agent who responds may not be the property's listing agent. We will explain the agent's role before you decide whether to work together.

**If lender help is offered and the operation supports independent choice:**

> You choose your lender. Tell us if you would like financing information. We disclose any business relationship with a lender we introduce.

**If the user explicitly wants an explanation of industry lawsuits:**

> Recent real estate platform lawsuits have raised questions about who receives buyer inquiries, how commercial relationships affect referrals, and how rental-advertising markets compete. The cases have different outcomes and stages; allegations should not be treated as established facts. Our approach is to make the recipient, agent's role, and any commercial relationship clear before you choose your next step.

Accompany the last paragraph with a dated, source-linked explanation if published; refresh statuses before release. Avoid placing a competitor-litigation narrative in the ordinary inquiry form when a direct recipient disclosure answers the user's actual question.

## Internal rationale and language traps

- “Good faith” is a standard of behavior, not a certification or immunity. Prefer specific, testable promises about identity, routing, choice, and correction.
- Do not say Zillow was “found guilty,” that allegations prove deception, or that a settlement proves liability. Do not turn a dismissal with leave to amend into final exoneration.
- Do not say this platform “avoids all lawsuits,” is “fully compliant,” is “legally protected,” or is “safer than Zillow” because it uses disclosures.
- Avoid “independent,” “unbiased,” “best agent,” or “matched for your needs” if sponsorship, referral economics, ownership, or availability determines the result. If advertising affects placement, label it near the placement and explain what it means.
- Avoid claiming that submitting a request can never create any legal relationship; agency can depend on subsequent conduct and applicable law. Describe the action as an inquiry and explain later agreements and actual roles.
- Distinguish seller/listing agent, buyer's agent, brokerage, portal, and lender. A seller's agent can answer questions without representing the buyer; a logo beside a listing does not establish agency.
- Check claims against real workflows: named recipient before submission, visible listing attribution, documented routing, consent appropriate to follow-up, separate lender choice, and a workable path to correct inaccurate information. These are recommendations inferred from the sources, not a legal safe harbor.

No website code, deployments, messages, or credentials were changed for this research.
