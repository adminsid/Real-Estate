# New York consumer confidence requirements

Verified: November 15, 2026. Scope: New York brokerage search, inquiries, showings, sales and NYC rentals. Legal sources below are NY DOS, NY Senate's current statutes/bill histories, and official NYC materials. A separately labeled OneKey source was added at the research lead's request. Product recommendations are implementation judgments, not additional statutory duties. This is a requirements research note, not a complete transaction-law review.

## Findings that change the product

**Do not tell every buyer that New York law requires signing a buyer representation agreement before touring.** No such enacted statewide requirement was located in current [Article 12-A](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/A12-A). The proposed requirement is [S9564](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9564), still in Senate Judiciary (March 25, 2026), and [A8910](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A8910), still in Assembly Judiciary (January 7, 2026). These are pending bills, not current law. A required agreement may instead arise from the actual brokerage's published procedures, a participation agreement or another applicable contract.

**Agency disclosure, representation contract, fair housing notice and rental fee disclosure are different documents with different triggers.** A basic inquiry must not silently become consent to representation or dual agency. The official agency forms explicitly say “THIS IS NOT A CONTRACT.” [Sale form](https://dos.ny.gov/buyer-and-seller-disclosure-form-english), [rental form](https://dos.ny.gov/landlord-and-tenant-disclosure-form-english).

**NYC FARE remains an enforced law in the current DCWP guidance**, effective June 11, 2025. A tenant cannot be forced to hire an agent to see or rent a particular apartment. Voluntarily engaging one's own tenant broker remains possible. [DCWP, updated June 2, 2026](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/FAQ-Broker-Fees.page).

## Property condition forms and dates

The $500 credit in lieu of disclosure ended **March 20, 2024**, per [DOS's March 18, 2024 notice](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/NYSDOS/bulletins/39132db). The old generic DOS form URL still exposes obsolete credit language; do not use it as the current form.

Use the [DOS form effective July 1, 2025](https://dos.ny.gov/property-condition-disclosure-statement-eff-7125), linked from [DOS Important Updates](https://dos.ny.gov/important-updates-licensing). The 2025 revision adds septic-system information. Senate's current §462 revision metadata says July 4, 2025; metadata is not a substitute for DOS's stated form-use date.

The current [§462 form](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/462) asks about FEMA floodplain, 100-year and 500-year hazard areas, insurance requirements, federal disaster aid, insurance policy, elevation certificate and flood claims; it also asks about standing water and water penetration/damage. Answers reflect seller's actual knowledge. “As is” is compatible with the statute and does not itself remove the disclosure requirement. Do not present a map-derived risk estimate as the seller's answer or imply a low-risk zone means no flooding.

## Fair housing and lawful source of income

Current [Executive Law §296(5)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/296) includes lawful source of income among protected housing characteristics and prohibits discriminatory availability statements, terms and advertising. Owner/room exemptions are fact-dependent; advertisement rules can affect an exemption. This note verifies statutory text, **not a final disposition of statewide Section 8 constitutional litigation** referenced in the older repository note. Obtain that court-status review before claiming a statewide repeal or universal statewide compulsion to participate in a particular program.

For NYC, [CCHR's housing-provider guidance](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/cchr/downloads/pdf/materials/FairHouse_FAQs-Landlord-English.pdf) says most rentals are covered; exceptions concern narrowly defined owner/family-occupied, unadvertised small buildings or rooms. It prohibits separate voucher inventory, selective acceptance of programs, and added income/guarantor requirements where the subsidy already calculates tenant contribution. It bars credit-history/score rejection when subsidy covers the full rent and requires individual consideration otherwise. [HRA](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hra/help/income-discrimination.page) flags refusing tours, demanding cash/security despite vouchers, and requiring a credit check to view/hold an apartment. HRA's shorter three-unit summary differs from CCHR's fuller exceptions discussion; do not auto-exempt solely from feed unit count.

Recommended UX: one housing inventory and equal inquiry/showing access; no “voucher-eligible only” section, employment-only filters, automatic 40× total-rent gate, or automatic credit-score disqualification. A voluntary private accommodation/assistance conversation must not publish medical or voucher documents. Program approval and unit eligibility remain separate factual questions from nondiscrimination.

## Private rules and unresolved release inputs

**NYC rental IDX requires a specific release decision.** [OneKey's FARE FAQ](https://support.onekeymls.com/hc/en-us/articles/38994162809236-Fairness-in-Apartment-Rental-Expenses-FARE-Act-Frequently-Asked-Questions), updated July 14, 2025, says passive IDX publication falls within the presumption and a publishing broker cannot charge the tenant. It also says affected rental listings were set to Internet=N and removed from IDX, third-party syndication and OneKey's consumer site, while clarification of passive publication was being sought. This is provider guidance and a dated system-status statement, not independent evidence that present feed access is authorized. Confirm the current feed entitlement and written provider instructions before displaying NYC rentals.

**Do not equate the fee rule with creation of agency.** The governing [FARE text, §20-699.21(e)](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=6557858&GUID=2E6273DC-FF0F-40B2-AAB5-B9B3D9BD09DB) presumes landlord permission/authorization to publish. Agency relationships and informed consent are addressed separately in the [DOS agency form](https://dos.ny.gov/landlord-and-tenant-disclosure-form-english). Product/legal inference: publishing may trigger fee and advertising obligations without an inquiry click creating consumer representation. Sending a consumer directly to the listing agent does not erase the publishing platform/broker's own duties. [DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/FAQ-Broker-Fees.page) expressly says a broker cannot require the tenant to hire that broker to see or rent the apartment; tenant-broker service advertising cannot condition identifiable inventory on hiring.

NAR/REALTOR membership, an MLS participant's agreement, and brokerage policy are distinct from NY licensure. Do not infer NAR membership or OneKey participation from a license or listing feed. Current NAR/OneKey touring agreements, compensation rules and open-house exceptions were **not independently verified** in this note. Obtain the actual applicable rules and signed participation/IDX terms before implementing a tour gate or asserting an industry obligation. Show the source of each requirement as law, applicable contract/MLS rule, or Prime America policy.

Brokerage SOP publication, prescribed form delivery, actual consent and record retention need an operating owner. Consumer-facing links and copy support those workflows; they do not prove compliance by themselves.
