# Prime America — Search trust, rights and language guide

Research date: October 8, 2026. Status: proposed brokerage policy and product requirements; not implemented or certified as a complete legal review. Initial market: all five NYC boroughs. Leadership names and business titles below were supplied by the company; license records have not been independently verified.

Related: [website vocabulary](website-vocabulary-guide.md), [NYC rental-rights research](nyc-rental-rights-research.md), [IDX display review](idx-preview-display-review.md). This guide adds safeguards to the vocabulary guide; it does not authorize disclosure of confidential or non-public MLS data.

## 1. Translate the philosophy into observable conduct

**Clearing obstacles from path to home** means helping people understand a property, reach the responsible professional, make informed choices and exercise their rights. The search service should demonstrate this through ordinary use, rather than requiring consumers to trust a slogan.

Proposed Prime America commitments:

- Show useful, permitted property information with sources, costs and uncertainty clearly explained.
- Identify the listing company and actual message recipient before a person sends an inquiry.
- Explain whom a professional represents; a contact button must not quietly enroll someone in representation.
- Offer consistent access to information, applications and showings. Measure unanswered inquiries and unequal delays as well as explicit refusals.
- Provide a usable path to the supervising brokerage and official complaint resources.
- Make requests, agreements, estimates and confirmations distinguishable.
- Improve information and service without fabricated urgency, paid replacement of the listing contact, or recommendations based on assumptions about who belongs in a neighborhood.

These are proposed company standards, not claims that every feature exists today.

## 2. Keep the authority behind each rule visible

| Classification | Meaning | Product consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal duty | Applicable enacted law, regulation, court order and jurisdiction-specific requirements | Record scope, authority, effective date, exceptions and review owner. A branding setting cannot remove it. |
| Industry or contractual rule | Applicable MLS/IDX agreement, participation rules or professional obligations | Verify each provider and membership relationship separately. Do not label a trade association rule as a statute. |
| Prime America standard | A company choice about clarity, fairness and service | Name it as our commitment and build the operation needed to deliver it. |

Article 12-A is a foundation for NY licensing, not the whole website compliance perimeter. The review must also address implementing regulations, fair housing, consumer protection, rental charges, accessibility, privacy, consent and transaction-specific disclosure duties. A conflict between a feed instruction and applicable law goes to the responsible broker and legal reviewer; neither a disclaimer nor silently changing the feed resolves it.

The DOS [license-law compilation](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law) is dated January 2024. Use it to locate provisions, then check amendments and current authoritative sources. Pending bills, agency guidance, contracts and court rulings need separate labels.

### Initial NY compliance checkpoints

| Topic | Verified foundation | Proposed website requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Licensing and supervision | Article 12-A; 19 NYCRR 175.21 requires regular, frequent and consistent broker oversight of associated salespersons | Verify active license and association before enabling brokerage representation; assign a supervising broker and review path. |
| Advertising | 19 NYCRR 175.25 addresses licensed identity, brokerage contact information, authorized advertising and truthful descriptions | Preserve required identity on search, detail, agent, embedded and shared pages; review public previews and messages too. |
| Fair housing notice | 19 NYCRR 175.28–29 addresses first substantive contact and a prominent homepage link | Provide the official notice with legally appropriate delivery and records; a footer or ordinary form checkbox is not proof of compliance. |
| Brokerage operating procedures | RPL 442-h and DOS guidance require publication of the broker's standardized operating procedures | Link the actual approved procedures, including whether identification, an exclusive agreement or preapproval is required; do not invent statutory prerequisites. |
| Agency disclosure | RPL 443 and the prescribed forms explain applicable agency relationships | Use the correct sale/rental form and timing; preserve its distinction from a representation contract. Do not assume its statutory scope covers every property type. |
| Feed permissions | Signed source-specific MLS/IDX contracts and rules | Public field/media permissions, attribution, notices, updates and sharing remain a separate production release review. Demo API access is not production authorization. |

Authorities: [DOS license-law compilation, sections 175.21, 175.28–29](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-license-law), [advertising regulations](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-advertising-regulations), [operating procedures](https://dos.ny.gov/standardized-operating-procedures), [agency form](https://dos.ny.gov/buyer-and-seller-disclosure-form-english). This table is a starting register, not an exhaustive Article 12-A audit.

Proposed responsibility assignments, subject to company confirmation and license verification:

| Surya Shrestha | CFO | Accurate fee presentation and financial controls with the licensed/legal review owners |
| Associated licensees | Actual current licensed names and classifications | Accurate listing/contact information, disclosures, consistent service and escalation of unlawful instructions |
| External listing companies | Clearly identified independent brokerage | Their listing representation and supervision; Prime America should not claim to supervise their agents |

Corporate titles do not establish real estate licensure. Use the exact licensed brokerage identity and required contact information, even when the search product has a separate brand. NY advertising rules prohibit substitute classifications such as “licensed sales agent” and “sales associate.” A verified example would be **Gobinda Lama — Licensed Real Estate Broker · Principal Broker**; associated salespersons use their actual classification. Do not publish these as verified credentials until records agree. [DOS advertising regulations](https://dos.ny.gov/real-estate-advertising-regulations).

Broker oversight is an operational obligation; do not promise that leadership personally reviews every message or guarantees every third-party fact. RPL 442-c has specific conditions governing responsibility for salesperson violations; avoid describing it as automatic liability for every act. [RPL 442-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/442-C).

## 4. Explain the connection before asking for information

Proposed listing contact presentation:

> Listing provided by [actual listing brokerage].
>
> [Licensed name and classification] — [verified representation role].
>
> Your message goes to [actual person/team and brokerage].

Use **Ask the listing agent** only when the operation actually delivers to that agent or an identified authorized listing team. If Prime America receives the message to help arrange contact, use **Ask Prime America about this property** and explain that route. Show a failure or fallback honestly; “received by Prime America” is not “delivered to the listing agent.”

Offer optional representation context: **I have an agent · I don't have an agent · I'm not sure**. This is routing information, not consent to hire anyone. Do not require a person to become a buyer/tenant client to make a basic inquiry. An optional offer to discuss representation should be a distinct action with its own explanation.

The NY disclosure form explains that a seller's agent serves the seller and that dual agency limits duties and requires informed written consent. The agency disclosure itself is not a contract. [Official agency form](https://dos.ny.gov/buyer-and-seller-disclosure-form-english).

Where applicable, written buyer agreements address services and compensation before touring with a professional working with the buyer. NAR distinguishes these from independently visiting an open house or asking about services; compensation is negotiable. Do not turn this into a universal claim that every visitor must hire an agent. Check the actual MLS participation rules and NY requirements for the relationship in question. [NAR consumer guide](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-to-written-buyer-agreements).

Keep agency disclosure, representation agreement, fair housing notice, inquiry processing and optional marketing consent separately named. A plain-language summary accompanies required documents; it does not replace them.

## 5. Put rights beside the decisions they affect

| Place | Consumer action | Operational counterpart |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search and rental details | Understand your rental rights | Jurisdiction-specific explanation and official resources; no separate voucher-only inventory |
| Costs | Understand rental costs | Verified payer, recipient, amount, frequency, legal basis and unknowns; no missing-value-to-zero conversion |
| Application information | Ask about application requirements | Reviewed criteria appropriate to the assistance/program and transaction; no universal automatic exclusion |
| Accessibility and pet policy | Request an accommodation | Private responsible contact and appropriate dialogue; no public medical-document upload requirement |
| Contact and tour request | See who will respond | Named route, representation role, honest delivery state and a clear way to include an existing agent |
| Every listing | Report a concern | Preserve relevant evidence, route to an accountable reviewer, and offer independent government channels |

NYC prohibits source-of-income discrimination in covered housing. That reaches conduct as well as advertisements. Program eligibility or approval is a different question from the right to nondiscriminatory treatment. Screening, fee and accommodation details, source links and applicability questions are documented in [the rental-rights research](nyc-rental-rights-research.md).

Two current issues require dated review: statewide Section 8 litigation is scheduled before the Court of Appeals on October 20, 2026; the May stay motion was denied as unnecessary, not a simple declaration that no stay exists. Do not publish a statewide repeal claim. [Court calendar](https://www.nycourts.gov/ctapps/calendar/2026/Monthly/Oct26CaseIssues.pdf), [motion decision](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/current/motions/2026/2026_68687.shtml). NYC agency summaries also differ on some small-owner coverage thresholds; do not automate exemptions from a unit-count field.

Current NYC DCWP guidance treats FARE as effective and enforced, with rental fee disclosures required in advertisements. Unknown required fees need resolution before publication; “not provided” is not a substitute for a legally required disclosure. [DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/FAQ-Broker-Fees.page). Detailed fee applicability and litigation limits remain in the research note.

Offer official paths to [NYC CCHR](https://www.nyc.gov/site/cchr/index.page), [NY DOS](https://dos.ny.gov/fair-housing), [DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/FAQ-Broker-Fees.page) and [NY Attorney General tenant resources](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/tenants-homeowners/tenants). An internal report is not a government filing. Do not require an internal complaint first or promise an official outcome.

## 6. Language examples with required behavior

These are proposed copy patterns. They are suitable only when the underlying facts, applicable law and operation support them. Words cannot repair discriminatory screening or misleading routing.

| Avoid or flag for review | Proposed wording or approach | Required behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “No Section 8,” “no programs” | “We consider all lawful sources of income without discrimination, including rental assistance.” | Carry that commitment through inquiries, showings and applications; check coverage without inventing exemptions. |
| “Working professionals only” | Describe the property's rooms, layout and features. | Do not use employment status as a proxy to exclude lawful income sources. |
| “Perfect for couples,” “no children” | “Two bedrooms and one bathroom,” if accurate. | Describe housing, rather than preferred residents. |
| “40× rent and 700 credit required for everyone” | “Ask about application requirements.” Publish reviewed criteria when available. | Handle assistance and tenant payment share appropriately; do not promise approval. |
| “No animals under any circumstances” | “Pet policy: [verified policy]. Disability-related accommodation requests are considered separately.” | Provide an actual accommodation process; distinguish assistance animals from ordinary pets. |
| “Safest neighborhood,” “the right people,” “best families” | Objective, dated, attributed location information selected by the consumer. | Avoid protected-class proxies and subjective belonging judgments in recommendations. |
| “Your agent,” before representation is established | “Listing agent — represents the [seller/landlord],” when verified. | Explain the relationship; do not infer agency from a click. |
| “Our listing,” on another company's listing | “Listing provided by [brokerage].” | Retain lawful authorization, source attribution and accurate identity. |
| “Free buyer agent,” “standard commission” | “Review services and compensation before agreeing.” | Explain any actual arrangement and payer; do not guarantee a third party will pay. |
| “No fee,” when only one fee is known | Name the specific verified fee and display other required charges. | Never imply zero total costs from an absent feed field. |
| “Book your tour,” without live confirmed access | “Request a tour.” | Distinguish received, forwarded, confirmed, canceled and failed states. |
| “Verified listing,” based only on feed ingestion | “Source: [MLS]. Listing updated [source date].” | Identify exactly what was checked, by whom and when; do not imply verified legal use or condition. |
| “Private and confidential,” for an anyone-with-link exclusive | “Anyone with this link can view and forward it.” | Only publish authorized content; prevent public indexing and unintended previews, while acknowledging forwarding is possible. |
| “Guaranteed return,” “approved conversion” | Source financial assumptions or actual documented approval, clearly identified. | Do not turn estimates, projected use or source claims into guarantees. |

Advertising guidance supports describing property features rather than discriminatory preferences. [NYC advertising examples](https://www.nyc.gov/site/fairhousing/rights-responsibilities/real-estate-advertisements.page). Photo truthfulness also matters: NY DOS specifically warns against misleading AI property imagery. Proposed policy: retain accurate originals, label staging and proposed changes, and review alterations that misrepresent actual condition; a label alone cannot cure a deceptive image. [DOS AI warning](https://dos.ny.gov/node/82971).

### Terms that need an explanation beside their use

| Term | Consumer explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Listing brokerage | The company identified as listing this property. Its representation role should be explained separately. |
| Buyer's agent / tenant's agent | A professional engaged to represent your interests under the actual arrangement. |
| Unrepresented | You have not engaged an agent to represent you in this transaction. It does not mean you lose fair treatment or housing rights. |
| Dual agency | One brokerage represents both sides under the legally required informed consent; explain the limits on duties. |
| Agency disclosure | Explains whom the professional represents. It is different from hiring that professional. |
| Representation agreement | Defines the services, duration, compensation and other terms you agree to. Read the actual agreement. |
| Rental assistance / voucher | A program may pay some or all rent. Program approval and your portion vary; assistance is not a reason for discriminatory treatment. |
| Reasonable accommodation | A requested change needed because of disability, reviewed under applicable standards. A request is not a guaranteed outcome. |
| Operating procedures | The brokerage's published requirements before working with prospective buyers; distinguish company choices from legal duties. |
| Exclusive listing | A verified marketing arrangement; it does not automatically mean unavailable on every other website. |
| REALTOR® | A membership designation, not a synonym for every licensed professional. Verify entitlement before use. |

For membership terminology see [NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-to-written-buyer-agreements). The general vocabulary guide covers ownership types, building charges and financing estimates.

## 7. Imported MLS language needs supervision too

Proposed moderation policy:

1. Flag potentially discriminatory or deceptive remarks, photo captions, generated descriptions and shared-page text before consumer publication. Automated flags are review prompts, not legal determinations; context, translations and exceptions matter.
2. Hold suspect content for the responsible broker's review. Ask the listing company to correct it through the appropriate source process. Determine whether the affected advertisement must be withheld while required fields and legal duties are resolved.
3. Preserve a restricted original and decision history where retention is lawful and necessary. Do not copy restricted text into public error messages or searchable audit files.
4. Do not silently rewrite source remarks, hide unfavorable lawful facts, or label unreviewed content compliant. Preserve legitimate facts and required attribution where publication is authorized and lawful.
5. Repeat the review for material changes. A feed disclaimer does not replace review of what this website publishes.

Apply the same policy to Prime America exclusives, external listings, syndication, email, social sharing, translations and AI summaries. A model must not invent representation, legal approvals, screening requirements, rights or fees. Sensitive accommodation and assistance information must not become public marketing or search-ranking signals.

## 8. Make supervision and compliance review maintainable

Proposed copy register fields: content identifier, approved phrase, meaning, prohibited substitutions, jurisdiction, property/transaction type, authority category, source URL, effective date, last checked date, responsible reviewer, status and next review trigger. Keep superseded versions for appropriate audit purposes. Legal review should resolve scope and exceptions before a rule is automated.

Proposed broker review process: identify concern → preserve appropriate evidence → assign responsible brokerage/reviewer → acknowledge honestly → investigate → correct the relevant listing or process → record outcome and follow-up. Provide a documented non-retaliation policy. Audit response times, routing failures, screening consistency and repeated complaints; do not train staff to identify or evade fair housing testers.

Records need a rule-specific retention and access schedule. For example, NY fair housing notice rules include three-year records, and NYC rental fee disclosures have their own signed-record obligations. Do not infer that every health document or message should be stored forever.

For a branded or embedded search service, test direct links, listing previews, search pages, mobile views and host pages. Required identities, notices, contact roles and protections must survive customization. Commercial, investment and residential layouts can differ without losing the applicable protections.

### Outstanding review before production

- Verify licensed company/person names, classifications, current associations and actual SOP; establish approved supervision and escalation ownership.
- Complete a provision-by-provision Article 12-A and NYCRR applicability register, beyond the initial checkpoints above.
- Resolve the dated voucher litigation/coverage issues and transaction-specific screening, fees, accommodations and criminal-history workflows with qualified NY review.
- Review executed OneKeyMLS, NYStateMLS and MLS Grid agreements and current rules separately; approve public field/media permissions and contact routing.
- Validate disclosures and actual service behavior; test that a request does not create undisclosed agency, marketing enrollment or a false confirmation.
- Complete remaining accessibility, privacy, communications, consumer-protection and property/transaction-specific compliance reviews. This guide does not certify those areas.

Expansion requires a separately reviewed jurisdiction profile for each state and country: local licensed entity, authorized roles, representation, rights, fees, required documents, privacy and language. Prime America's values can remain consistent; NY credentials and rules cannot be presented as authorization to practice everywhere.
