# NYC real estate product and marketing playbook

Use the [website vocabulary and presentation guide](website-vocabulary-guide.md) for consumer labels, buttons, explanations, and visual starting standards.

Date: October 7, 2026. Proposed operating plan; campaigns have not been published and consumer experiments have not been run. Read the linked [consumer decision research](real-estate-consumer-psychology.md) for evidence, primary sources, and limitations.

## Positioning to test

Find NYC homes with clearer costs, real layouts, verified building facts, and a local team that follows through. This is the intended service promise, not a claim that these capabilities are all implemented today. Launch copy must describe only delivered capabilities.

The practical competition is the entire decision process: a shopper should be able to find a relevant home, see whether it fits, identify unresolved questions, and get a useful answer. More clicks, more leads, or a prettier gallery alone do not prove improvement. Zillow's current AI search, comparison, saved searches, and tour actions are competitive baselines.

## Audience needs, without demographic steering

Design around declared tasks: buying a first home, evaluating a condo/co-op, renting, buying a multi-family property, relocating, or selling. Ask what the person needs from the property. Do not infer preferences from protected characteristics, demographic neighborhood profiles, or supposed personality types. Five-borough service means searchable coverage and measured service capacity across all five boroughs, not five interchangeable landing pages.

## Acquisition and conversion system

| Channel / entry point | Useful promise | Destination and next action | Success measure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Organic listing search | Specific available properties with reliable facts | A current Listing page or preserved matching search | Relevant listing visits and suitable shortlists; expired pages have honest status |
| Borough/neighborhood guides | Objective housing types, building considerations, and practical search options | Useful guide with current matching inventory | Searches started with understood tradeoffs; coverage gaps visible |
| Co-op/condo explainers | Understand recurring fees and documents before a tour | Sourced guide, then relevant property-type search | Fewer unresolved basic policy/cost questions |
| Owned exclusive media | Understand the actual property and layout | Public canonical Listing; privately shared Listing only through its authorized link | Informed inquiries and completed tours |
| Social photo/video series | Answer one property question accurately | The exact Listing or equivalent saved filters | Qualified visits, not views alone |
| Saved-search notifications | See a relevant new match or material change at chosen cadence | Matching Listing plus why it matches | Useful returns; opt-outs and stale notifications are guardrails |
| Seller case studies | See real preparation, media quality, and follow-through | Evidence-backed service page and seller consultation request | Completed qualified seller appointments and signed engagements |

Do not publish demo inventory as actual offers. Do not distribute MLS photos to social or paid advertising merely because they are permitted on an IDX site; verify the use rights. Marketing consent and listing inquiry consent remain separate.

## Listing media production brief

Each exclusive gets a media checklist before launch:

- Rights, source, capture date, Listing identity, and public/private sharing decision.
- Exterior/arrival, main living space, kitchen, bedrooms, bathrooms, building amenities, and outdoor space where applicable.
- Accurate floor plan where available, with orientation and readable labels. Dimensions are verified or explicitly identified as supplied estimates.
- Full authorized gallery, ordered to explain the property. Keep originals and original provider ordering available; remove duplicates without obscuring condition.
- Factual room captions. Do not label a room or amenity based on a guess. Staged imagery, when permitted, keeps its disclosure.
- A short walkthrough for layout, a separate factual clip addressing one useful question, and an accessible text explanation. No automatic audio or fabricated visuals.
- Media and links rechecked after status, permission, or material fact changes.

Example content briefs (templates, not claims about current Listings): “Walk from the entrance to the living room,” “What this floor plan shows,” “What this building's verified maintenance includes,” and “Three unanswered questions to confirm before your tour.”

## Homepage and Listing page priorities

Consumers control information depth. Offer a readable overview plus full consumer-permitted details; never withhold permitted facts behind a contact form or replace source facts with an opaque marketing summary. Good agent service earns its place through verification and execution, rather than exclusive access to ordinary property information. See [the transparency requirement](nyc-property-search-spec.md#consumer-information-transparency).

The homepage should lead with search, usable filters, and clear geographic scope. Then show real available Listings, how the local service works, and recent attributable evidence. Academy promotion belongs where people seeking licensing education can find it without dominating consumer discovery.

The Listing page should answer, in order: what is it, where is it, what does the space look like, what does it cost, what important facts are known or missing, and what happens if I ask a question. Put source and update time near the facts. Distinguish “request a tour” from a confirmed appointment. Avoid phone-number capture as a gate to browsing.

Example launch-safe headings depend on readiness:

- Search: “Find your next place in New York City.” Only use after five-borough inventory coverage is verified.
- Media: “Explore the property.” Show accurate photos and available floor plans.
- Questions: “What would you like to know?” Show the actual recipient and response process once routing works.
- Seller: “See how we prepare and market your property.” Support it with real work examples and a clear service plan.

## Inquiry to post-close operations

1. A submitted question/tour request records Inquiry Attribution, chosen channel, consent, and a responsible agent. Acknowledge only durable receipt; do not imply the appointment is confirmed.
2. Agent triage checks current availability and the question's scope. Assign a backup owner when needed. Establish an achievable coverage schedule before publishing any response-time promise.
3. The response answers the actual question, labels missing facts, and gives a concrete next step. It does not automatically enroll the person into a campaign.
4. Tour confirmation records the verified appointment, access arrangement, responsible agent, cancellation path, and questions to investigate. Notes after the tour update the shopper's shortlist with permission.
5. Offer and transaction communication follows verified milestones and responsible professionals. Buyer-facing updates show next actions and dates; avoid invented urgency or unsupported acceptance probabilities.
6. Closing and post-close follow-up reconcile final responsibilities, provide useful next steps, and seek optional feedback. Review requests follow service; referrals and marketing require the appropriate permission.

## Proposed release sequence

**Before live feed approval:** media replication and gallery testing on demo inventory; factual media brief for exclusives; task-based consumer interviews; search and inquiry implementation; staging review. No paid promotion to unfinished consumer flows.

**First consumer release:** approved current inventory, accurate images, five-borough search coverage, source/freshness states, accessible Listing details, actual inquiry delivery, responsible agent ownership, and private-link access controls. Define service capacity and instrument completion/failure states.

**Next release:** verified condo/co-op information and transparent cost components; shortlist and shared comparison; opt-in change alerts. Prioritize the missing facts research participants repeatedly need, rather than adding a long speculative feature list.

**Growth phase:** only after the consumer flow and follow-through work, test specific public exclusive campaigns, useful local guides, and seller case studies. Add paid acquisition after approval of its budget, destinations, and claims. Expand successful topics according to qualified outcomes, not impressions alone.

## Measurement and competitive proof

Create a task scorecard before implementation: find a suitable Listing, identify layout, explain monthly components and unknowns, compare candidates, get a factual answer, and arrange a confirmed tour. Run equivalent tasks against the current site and Zillow when equivalent inventory exists. Record data differences separately from interface differences.

Track technical measures (freshness, failures, accessibility, missing media), consumer measures (task success, decision understanding, useful return visits), and service measures (question resolution, response time distribution, tour confirmation/completion). Follow unsubscribe and unwanted-contact reports as guardrails.

For each of the ten hypotheses in the research document, choose one primary metric and a practical discovery/pilot experiment. Recruit 12–15 task-based discovery participants as proposed exploratory research, not a representative sample. Predefine power/sample size and stop rules before later A/B tests; do not advertise unmeasured conversion or superiority claims.

## Definition of being ahead

A defensible advantage exists when consumers repeatedly complete a relevant task more successfully, with fewer unresolved facts and better follow-through, under comparable conditions. The current deliverables establish media plumbing and a research-backed plan. They do not establish an advantage over Zillow yet.
